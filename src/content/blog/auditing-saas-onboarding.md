Most onboarding problems are misdiagnosed. Teams see trial abandonment and reach for a product
tour, when the real culprit is usually something structurally quieter: a question asked too
early, an empty state with no next move, or a first session that demands effort before
demonstrating value. This framework is the audit sequence I use to find the actual failure
point before proposing any fix.

## Outline

### 1. Start with the funnel, not the screens

- Locate the sharpest drop: sign-up → first session → second session → activation event
- Why "activation event" must be defined before the audit means anything
- Cohort views over aggregates: recent changes hide in averages

### 2. Define the first "aha" — precisely

- The outcome a new user must experience (not a feature they must configure)
- How to derive it from retained-user behaviour when the team can't agree

### 3. Walk the first session like a new user

- The clean-account walkthrough: record it, timestamp every ask vs. every payoff
- The "effort ledger": what the product asks for vs. what it gives, in order
- Auditing the invited-teammate path separately (almost nobody does)

### 4. Audit the empty states

- Every empty state is an onboarding screen whether the team meant it or not
- The three questions each one must answer: what is this, why is it empty, what now

### 5. Cross-check with humans

- Five trial-abandoner interviews beat fifty survey responses
- Support tickets and session recordings as cheap triangulation

### 6. Turn findings into a prioritised fix list

- Severity × reach × effort, with the activation metric as the tiebreaker
- Why "add a tour" is almost never in the top five
