Remote design work fails quietly. Nobody tells you the developers have been guessing at edge
cases, or that the PM stopped looping you in because your feedback arrived too late to matter.
By the time it's visible, you're "the designer who throws files over the wall." The fix isn't
more meetings — it's a small set of habits that make your thinking legible while you're not in
the room. These are the ones that have mattered most in my remote work.

## Outline

### 1. The failure mode: invisible until it isn't

- How remote designers get routed around, one small skip at a time
- Presence is an output of habits, not hours online

### 2. Work in public by default

- Sharing in-progress Figma with narration comments, not big reveals
- The weekly written update format (what moved, what's blocked, what I need)

### 3. Decision records: the highest-leverage document

- A five-line format: decision, alternatives, reason, owner, date
- Why they eliminate the worst remote failure — re-litigating settled questions

### 4. Working with PMs across time zones

- Async-first triage: what genuinely needs a call vs. a Loom vs. a comment
- Disagreeing productively in writing without escalation theatre

### 5. Handoff as a conversation, not a file drop

- Annotating intent and edge cases, not just measurements
- The implementation-review pass: cheap, and developers actually like it

### 6. The overlap hours that count

- Protecting critique and decision meetings; letting everything else be async
