I worked as the first and only designer on Digitt+, a fintech app built for financial inclusion
in Pakistan's agri-sector and rural economy — owning research, information architecture, UX, and
visual design across iOS and Android, including building the product's design system from
scratch.

## At a glance

- **Sole designer on the team** — owned research through visual design, end to end
- Designed for a **low-literacy, low-tech-familiarity user base**, including Urdu localization
  and hands-free audio guidance
- Built the **full design system from scratch**: color, typography, iconography, illustration,
  and component states
- Designed the core app plus exploratory features for a **platform expansion** outside the
  original roadmap

## The context

Digitt+ is a digital financial services product built for financial inclusion in Pakistan's
agri-sector and rural economy; the company holds In-Principle approval from the State Bank of
Pakistan to operate as an Electronic Money Institution.

The workforce it serves is underserved by existing players — EasyPaisa, JazzCash, Abhi, and
SadaPay all address the broader market, but not this audience's specific constraints: limited
formal education, low familiarity with digital tools, language barriers, and physically
demanding work environments where fast, reliable access to wages matters most.

**My role:** Research, information architecture, UX, and visual design. I was the first and only
designer on the team, working directly with end-users, developers, and finance experts — owning
the core iOS/Android app end-to-end, plus designing features for a possible platform expansion
that wasn't on the original roadmap.

## The problem

The agriculture industry lacked specialized fintech built for its workforce: limited formal
education and digital literacy made most existing apps hard to use, language was a real barrier
without Urdu support, and demanding physical work environments made screen-heavy, text-heavy
interfaces impractical. Fast, reliable access to wages was a baseline requirement, not a
nice-to-have.

## Key decisions

- **Simplified the flow for lower-literacy users** — reduced navigation depth and leaned on
  clear visuals over text wherever possible
- **Built Urdu localization from the start**, not as a later add-on
- **Added hands-free audio guidance** for use in physical work environments — most competitor
  apps in this space don't offer this
- **Designed quick-action shortcuts** so core tasks — checking balance, sending money — took
  minimal steps
- **Started in low-fidelity** to pressure-test structure and scalability before committing to
  visual design, then refined based on what testing surfaced
- **Built the design system in parallel with the product, not after it** — tokens, type scale,
  color, iconography, illustration style, and component states (including error states like
  incorrect PIN entry) — so the product could scale to new screens, and to the
  platform-expansion work, without visual debt

## What shipped

A full iOS/Android app covering wallet registration and CNIC verification, dashboard and
transaction views, a services menu, and confirmation states, plus virtual and physical card
issuance — backed by a complete design system built to support the product beyond its initial
scope.

## Impact

Digitt+ successfully launched into a highly specific and underserved market, gaining strong
early adoption among Pakistan's agri-sector and rural workforce. Its simplified navigation, Urdu
localization, audio guidance, and quick-access financial actions made the product more
approachable for users who were often excluded by conventional fintech experiences.

The positive response from this niche customer base validated the need for a financial product
designed around their real literacy, language, and working-environment constraints, not merely
adapted from a mainstream banking app. The scalable design system also gave the team a
consistent foundation for expanding the product, introducing new services, and supporting future
platform growth without compromising usability.
